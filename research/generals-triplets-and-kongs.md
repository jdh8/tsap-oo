# Three and four identical generals (將帥)

Research checked 14 November 2026. The question: how many 胡 do several
identical generals — same colour, so 帥帥帥 or 將將將將 — score in 十胡?
Three held concealed, three with one claimed (if a general can be ponged at
all), and four, concealed or konged.  Several versions circulate; which
exist, who states each, and which is mainstream?

## Answer

Every source agrees on the base: **a general is a meld by itself worth 1 胡**,
and generals are not discarded.  No source distinguishes 紅帥, or 帥 from 將,
in scoring.  Beyond that there are four variants.

| | Variant | Three in hand | Three, one claimed | Four in hand | Four, konged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Generals add; a quad is an ordinary quad | 3 (3 × 1) | no pong | 8 | 6 |
| B | Generals add; the quad is inverted | 3 (3 × 1) | no pong | **6** | **8** |
| C | Generals add; the quad is a flat 8 | 3 (3 × 1) | no pong | 8 | 8 |
| D | Premium generals | **6** | **4** (pong allowed, "有的地方不能碰") | **10** | **8** |

Who backs each, counting text lineages rather than pages (most pages copy
one another):

- **A** — three lineages: a 2009 family write-up ([瓶子][ccukor]), English
  Wikipedia, and Chinese Wikipedia by silence (its table has one 明槓 6 /
  暗槓 8 row and its general rule names no exception).
- **B** — one lineage with two early publishers, the card maker
  [國瑜企業社][guoyu] and the online game [大蕃薯][gamebase]; whichever
  copied the other, UpToGo, two 痞客邦 posts and a reverted 2019 revision
  of Chinese Wikipedia copy it in turn.  The Chinese
  [Board Game Arena page][bga-zh] gives the same 6 / 8 and may or may not
  derive from it.
- **C** — two lineages: the English [Board Game Arena page][bga] and the
  family rules of [T. N. Truong][tnt] (region not stated).
- **D** — one dated lineage, a [2007 blog][max] copied by two 痞客邦 posts,
  plus one table shared by two recent casino-affiliate tutorials
  ([wm][wm], [wabo][wabo]) whose origin is unknown.

**Mainstream.**

- *Three generals:* 3 胡, as three singles, never ponged.  Variants A, B and
  C agree, and Chinese Wikipedia, both Board Game Arena pages, 瓶子 and
  Truong all forbid the pong outright.  Confidence: fairly high.  Variant D's
  6 / 4 is a real but minority table rule; its own source concedes the pong
  is not allowed everywhere.
- *Four generals:* no consensus.  The best-attested single number is **8 for
  the konged quad** — B, C and D all give it, against A's 6.  The concealed
  quad is 6 (B), 8 (A, C) or 10 (D).  Confidence that any one of these is
  "the" rule: low.  Every variant scores four generals above the 4 胡 of
  four singles, except one footnote in the B text that contradicts its own
  table (see Caveats).
- *Kong:* the fourth general is konged, by force, in every Taiwanese source
  that addresses it.  English Wikipedia notes variants that forbid stealing
  a general even for a quad.

**Assessment (mine, not a source's).**  Variant A is the best designed for
the triplet and defensible for the quad.

- A general cannot be discarded or ponged, so a set of them is dealt or
  drawn, never built.  Variant D pays 6 胡 — more than half a win — for a
  dealt triplet the player did nothing to get, and it makes three generals
  worth twice any other concealed triplet.  "Generals add" gives exactly the
  3 胡 of an ordinary concealed triplet, so nothing special is needed.
- For the quad, A reuses the ordinary 8 / 6 and needs no general-specific
  entry.  C's flat 8 is the simplest alternative and has an argument: a
  konged quad normally scores less because it is built on a claim, but a
  general quad is always a concealed triplet plus the one card that had to
  come anyway.
- B's inversion, where the dealt quad scores *less* than the konged one and
  less than any other dealt quad, has no stated rationale in any source.
  It reads like two swapped rows, but Board Game Arena implements it as
  written, so it cannot be dismissed as a typo.

**tsap-oo today** is variant A.  The konged general quad at 6 is the one
value where the repo sides with the minority (A alone) against 8 (B, C, D).

## Other general-specific rules found

- **目 parity (台中).**  The annotations in 河洛人 and 屏少的家: 「將(帥)士(仕)
  象(相)稱為"一目" , 單數"目", 10胡可臵, 雙數"目"或沒有"目"則要11胡才能臵」.
  It counts 將士象 sets, not generals, and moves the threshold, not any meld
  value.  This is presumably the "將 parity rule" the README mentions.
- **Odd totals.**  Huang, Truong and 金門日報 all say a correct winning total
  is always odd.  That is a checksum of their scales, in which every meld's
  value has the parity of its card count and a hand is 21 cards; it does
  not hold for the Taiwanese table (將士象 2, four soldiers 5) and is not a
  rule about generals.
- **Four colours of generals.**  瓶子 pays one 台 (a stake unit, not 胡) for
  「四種顏色的將帥」.  No other source has it.
- **Colour.**  Chinese Wikipedia ranks 帥 above 將 and 黃>紅>綠>白, but only
  for drawing seats.

## Caveats

- Read verbatim, from the raw page or wikitext: every source in the table
  except pagat, which came through a summarising fetch and has nothing on
  the question.  The Huang and 大蕃薯 pages are Wayback Machine copies.
- The 國瑜 footnote 「四隻同色的將 = 4 胡」 contradicts its own 6 / 8 rows.
  大蕃薯 carries the same table but says four generals score "以槓來計算",
  so the footnote may be a simplification, or may mean four generals of
  which none was konged.  No source settles it.
- Variant B's direction of copying is unknown.  The 國瑜 text has online-game
  wording (「輸每一家﹝10胡 x 押點﹞的點數」), which suggests the card maker
  copied a game's help page rather than the reverse; that is an inference.
- Not found or not opened: rule pages for 神來也, 宏碁戲谷 or Gamesofa; any
  PTT, Dcard or Mobile01 thread; anything in 國家文化記憶庫 or 台灣大百科.
  The MBM page returned no body text, games.porg.es returned 404, and the
  Baidu 十胡 page was empty.  "No source found" for these, not "no rule".
- wm and wabo are casino-affiliate content of unknown authorship; their
  table agrees with the 2007 blog on generals but not elsewhere (wm gives an
  ordinary quad 4 / 6).  Weak evidence that variant D has a second origin.
- Chinese Wikipedia's general sentence is unsourced, and its 明槓 / 暗槓 rows
  cite 大蕃薯 while omitting that page's separate general rows; reading it
  as variant A is an argument from silence.
- The Kinmen and Vietnamese rows are neighbouring games on a different
  scale.  They show the 3 / 6 / 8 ladder is shared, nothing more.

## In the code

- A general is `Meld::Single`, 1 胡 concealed or exposed (`Rules::new`).
- Three generals in hand score 3: the solver may lay them out as three
  singles or as a concealed triplet, and both are 3.  Winning on a third
  general scores the claimed card as an exposed single, 1 + 1 + 1, since
  `Round::win` takes the best completion and an exposed triplet is only 1.
- A general is never ponged or eaten into a pair (`options_for`), and a
  drawn general that nobody wins on or kongs goes into its drawer's hand
  (`Event::Keep`).
- The fourth general is a forced kong for whoever holds three, the drawer
  included, and scores as an exposed quad, 6 (`resolve`, before the keep).
- Four generals in hand can therefore only be dealt; the solver scores them
  as a concealed quad, 8.

Variants B, C and D each need a general-specific quad value, and D a
general-specific triplet and pong; `susik::Values` has one `triplet` and one
`quad` per side, so none of them is reachable by changing fields of `Rules`.
